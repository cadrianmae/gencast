Round-table speaker positioning — which geometry?

N=4 speakers shown for each. The listener is the orange dot, looking up the page. Click an option to choose.

AFront arc (industry standard)
All speakers in a front arc (typically ±60°). Listener never has voices behind them.
Pros: never disorienting, mixes always translate to any headphone/speaker, matches how most podcast/film audio is mixed.
Cons: doesn't feel like an actual table — more like a stage where speakers face the audience.
BListener at center, speakers around
Listener sits at the table's center; speakers radiate around them at full 360°.
Pros: real round-table feeling, fully immersive.
Cons: speakers behind listener (orange dots) confuse direction on bad headphones, weak HRTFs muddle front/back.
CListener as a seat at the table
Listener is one seat at the table; speakers occupy the other N seats. Forward-table seats face the listener; same-side seats are at ±90°+.
Pros: literally a round table; feels like being a participant.
Cons: with even spacing, some speakers end up at your sides or slightly behind. Same disorientation risk as B with weak HRTFs. Geometry math is non-trivial.

Hybrid worth considering

D

Front-half-of-round-table (recommended-leaning)

Like A, but spread the speakers across a wider front arc (e.g. ±90° = full front semicircle) so it feels more "around me" without putting anyone behind. N=2: ±45°, N=3: ±60°, ±0°, N=4: ±75°, ±25°.

Pros: feels more table-like than A; never behind; works with any HRTF dataset; predictable.

E

Configurable arc width per profile

Profile YAML carries arc_degrees: 120 (defaults to 120° = ±60°). Users who want immersion can set arc_degrees: 270 for near-full-circle. arc_degrees: 360 = true round-table B.

Pros: ships A as default, but B is one config knob away.
